Giving medicine safely

  • Use the syringe or cup that came with the medicine. Kitchen spoons are not accurate.
  • Check the strength on the box. The doses above only work for the strengths listed. Store brands are fine if the strength matches.
  • Read the labels of cold and flu products. Many already contain acetaminophen (Tylenol). Do not double up.
  • Do not switch back and forth between Tylenol and Motrin unless your provider tells you to.
  • Never give aspirin to children or teens.
  • The goal is comfort, not a normal temperature. Let a sleeping child sleep.

Call us or get care right away if

  • Your baby is under 3 months with a temperature of 100.4°F or higher
  • Fever is 104°F or higher, or lasts more than 3 days (more than 2 days if under age 2)
  • Trouble breathing, a stiff neck, a seizure, or a rash that looks like bruises or tiny dots
  • Very sleepy, hard to wake, or will not stop crying
  • Not drinking, or fewer wet diapers (fewer than 3 in 24 hours)
  • You think you gave too much medicine. Call Poison Control at 1‑800‑222‑1222, open 24 hours.

Bottom line: Give the amount for your child's weight, wait the full time between doses, and stay under the daily limit. Doses change as your child grows, so ask us for an updated sheet if their weight changes.
Doses follow standard pediatric weight-based dosing: acetaminophen 10 to 15 mg/kg every 4 to 6 hours (maximum 5 doses, 75 mg/kg, or 4,000 mg per day) and ibuprofen 5 to 10 mg/kg every 6 to 8 hours (maximum 4 doses or 40 mg/kg per day), for children 6 months and older.
